      Before you load up — read this

      This program is built around machines and cables specifically to keep load controlled and joints supported. Confirm your current diagnosis and any exercise restrictions with a doctor or physical therapist before progressing weight, especially around your back and shoulders. Recomp Coach isn't a medical provider.

      Back safety

      • Avoid heavy deadlifts, good mornings, heavy unsupported bent-over rows, loaded spinal flexion, and high-rep sit-ups for now.
      • On leg press, don't descend so deep that your lower back rounds off the pad — stop the rep just before that point.
      • Favor chest-supported rows, controlled machines, and pain-free range of motion over unstable, heavy free-weight positions.
      • Stop and get evaluated for: sharp or worsening pain, numbness, tingling, weakness, pain radiating down the leg, or any bowel/bladder changes.

      Shoulder safety

      • Favor neutral grips, machines, and cables with a controlled range of motion.
      • Avoid heavy overhead pressing, behind-the-neck pressing, heavy upright rows, heavy dips, and heavy shrugs for now.
      • Never push through significant pain — discomfort that lingers or sharpens is a stop signal, not something to work around.

      Progressive overload

      Use double progression. If an exercise is 3×8–12: week 1 might be 10/10/9, next session 10/10/10, then work up toward 12/12/12. Once all sets hit the top of the rep range with clean form, add the smallest practical amount of weight and start the climb again. Keep 2–3 reps in reserve on most sets, and never trade form for a heavier number.

      Recovery

      Aim for a 7–9 hour sleep window even if the sleep itself gets interrupted — total recovery matters more than one perfect night. If sleep was poor, dial back training volume or intensity that day. On a rough day, a walk or light mobility work is a completely valid substitute for a hard session. Stress management counts as training too.

      12-week roadmap

      Wk 1–2
      Build consistency — show up, learn the movements, track everything.
      Wk 3–4
      Improve training performance — better bar/machine feel, tighter execution.
      Wk 5–8
      Increase progressive overload — this is where most of the strength comes from.
      Wk 9–12
      Continue recomposition, then evaluate waist and strength changes.

      Steps & cardio

      Step target starts around 6,000/day, moves to 7,000–8,000 in weeks 3–6, and up to 8,000–10,000 later if recovery allows. Add 15–20 minutes of cardio on Friday.
